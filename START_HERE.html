The evidence behind a proposed customs code

HSGraph links products in a public production database to Harmonized System (HS) customs codes, and keeps the evidence, the checks and the gaps next to every proposed code. This page shows ten real records from the published dataset.

What we are asking. Spend five minutes on one record and tell us whether this would help you in a real task. You are not being asked to certify a classification or to review all ten.

Start with one exampleHow it worksThe ten recordsFeedbackData and terms

HS code. The Harmonized System is the international numbering used on customs declarations to say what a product is. Four digits name a heading, six a subheading.

USLCI record. The U.S. Life Cycle Inventory Database describes industrial processes and the products flowing into and out of them. A record here is one such flow in one documented process, not every product with that name.

Five minutes, one record

  1. Read the steel example below. Two codes are possible, and one missing fact would decide it.
  2. Open record R01 and check that the saved findings say what is missing. We did not fill the gap or choose a code.
  3. Tell us whether that would help you, using the reply template. No specialist credentials are needed to comment on clarity.

Have five more minutes? R02 shows the one owner decision in the dataset and its limits. R05 shows two model passes agreeing while the record still carries a conflict.

One example: a stainless-steel coil

Every row on this page works the same way: a source record, the codes that could apply, the fact that would decide, and what was recorded when that fact was missing.

How HSGraph keeps evidence, checks and opinions apart

Each record carries up to four layers. They are stored separately. A later layer never rewrites an earlier one, and agreement between layers is not proof.

1. Source record

What the USLCI process says about the product: its name, direction (input or output) and amount.

Steel exampleSteel; stainless 304; flat rolled coil

2. Rule check

Recorded classification rules applied to the record. Ends in one of the four outcomes below.

Steel exampleMore than one candidate

3. Model assessment

Two saved AI passes, an assessor and a challenger, each with an outcome, the facts it found missing and any concerns. Saved once; never re-run for this page.

Steel exampleassessor: competing targets; challenger: competing targets

4. Human review

An owner decision by the project owner, or a specialist validation by a classification expert. Across the whole dataset: one owner decision, zero specialist validations.

Steel exampleNone

The four rule-check outcomes

  • More than one candidateThe saved rules leave two or more codes possible; no single code is established.3 of these 10 records
  • Recorded conditions passThe saved rule checks pass for the proposed code. This is not specialist validation.3 of these 10 records
  • Information missingThe record lacks facts the saved checks need to settle the proposal.2 of these 10 records
  • Recorded conflictThe saved checks flag a conflict. Do not treat the proposed code as settled.2 of these 10 records

Record R05 · Compare: agreement is not correctness

Ethylene glycol; at plant

Recorded conflictNo specialist validation

This record is a small ethylene glycol input to a chlorine-making process, and the source note says it stands in for propylene glycol. So the record names one chemical and represents another. The saved passes agreed on 290531 for ethylene glycol, but the record keeps a concern that they classified the stand-in rather than the product it represents; the rule check records a conflict and the product identity is marked disputed. Two passes agreeing is not correctness, and this row shows why.

Your question: Does keeping this concern visible help you avoid trusting an unsuitable proposal?

What the full dataset contains

The ten records above come from HSGraph v0.1.0, published on Zenodo with a DOI. Counts are taken from its data card.

  • 6,936HS codes (HS 2022)
  • 1,422USLCI processes
  • 76,211recorded inputs and outputs
  • 670product records assessed
  • 1owner-accepted mapping
  • 0specialist validations

Every assessment in the dataset is an automated proposal with its evidence attached. The one owner acceptance is the cement record shown in R02.

What this is not

  • Not a customs ruling or classification advice. Codes here are saved candidates.
  • Not a verified supply chain or bill of materials. No supplier link is claimed.
  • Not a complete life-cycle inventory. The full dataset keeps most rows but leaves conversions and formulas unresolved.
  • Ten hand-picked records, not representative of the dataset.
  • Zero specialist validations so far. Saved AI findings are recorded outputs, not verification, and one owner acceptance is not expert review.

What we need back from you

One short reply is enough. “I cannot see a use for this” is useful feedback too. You do not need to classify the product, validate all ten rows, or endorse the project.

Reply to the person who sent you this page, or email rbedekar@zeroinsec.com. The link opens your own mail app; this page does not submit or store anything. Please do not send confidential product data.
